Step 3. The Scale Works for Big Diet

Before. “Hi Teddy! It’s been nine days since your last weigh-in. Small steps add up. Tap to step on.” That came in at 7:40 on a Saturday night in January, in the booth at Helen’s, while Britt was reading her own birthday card out loud to the rest of us.

After. I sued the scale in small claims, and the hearing sat down in the booth at Helen’s because the courthouse owned the same model and the judge would not let a cousin plug in while the family was on trial.

Step 17. Gravy Is a Medium

Before. On the Monday after Easter, the jar on our doormat had no meatballs in it.

After. By June the alley behind Pell’s building ran a gravy market, with posted prices in favors, and a man from the next block tried to pass a thinner jar until the block association seized it as contraband.

Step 14. Discipline Has a PR Team

Before. He produced a jeweler’s scale from his running belt, the kind that weighs a coin. The meatball did not fit on it.

After. The fasting coach came to correct our Sunday dinner, and by Thursday he had a drawer, a key, and a place at the table he had not agreed to, because Pell had adopted him the way she adopts a pot someone tried to throw out.

Step 7. Your Belt Is Lying

Before. One belt, three holes, two dollars a hole, the third hole free, four dollars total.

After. Father Ellis punched the new hole during the nine o’clock, with the choir holding the belt taut like a banner, and the pencil receipt Ray wrote afterward forbade that belt from ever coming back.

Step 5. The Belly Is Furniture

Before. The suit came in a garment bag from the closet at the bottom of the church basement stairs.

After. The shop required the foam, the children sat on me instead, Eddie came to take the sack, Nia wrote declined on his form, and we paid the two hundred so the pillow could stay on the hook unworn.

Step 9. Mass Is Presence

Before. Teddy, you’re showing, somebody whispered from behind the couch, and twenty adults in a dark living room laughed as quietly as they could.

After. A guest called the curtain a second room, the manager priced that caption at two hundred dollars a month, he voided it when he saw the shoes were on a person, the computer billed us anyway, and we stood at the counter until the line was deleted.
